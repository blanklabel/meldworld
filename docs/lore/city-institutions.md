# The Last City — institutions, infrastructure, and the underworld

> The city's **non-class** organisations: who governs, who polices, who carries the
> messages, who holds the Wall, and who runs the things the city would rather not admit
> it runs. The **orders a hero belongs to** — the ones with rank ladders and class keys —
> live in [`factions.md`](factions.md); the physical world they sit in is
> [`shifting-lands.md`](shifting-lands.md).
>
> **Status:** transcribed from design notes. Entries below vary from fully written to a
> single line, and the thin ones are marked **stub** rather than padded out — an invented
> paragraph in a canon file is worse than an honest gap. Nothing here is implemented; a
> few entries name things the game *does* have, and those are called out.

---

## Government

The city is governed by a coalition — the **First Bloc** — seated in the **Crystal
Tower**. Six peoples hold seats: **humans, elves, gnomes, goliaths, dwarves,
hobgoblins**.

**Stub / open:** the Explorers are described in [`factions.md`](factions.md) as
"government-funded, reporting to **the council**." Whether the council and the First Bloc
are the same body, or the council is a chamber within it, is unresolved.

## Law enforcement — the Sentinels

Run by the sentient guardian **"Steele"**. The Sentinels' retinue is constructed rather
than recruited: chain brutes, stone defenders, animated armor. *(The notes stat these
from D&D references; the creature names are canon, the stat blocks are tabletop-only.)*

### The guardian principle

**The city hands its incorruptible institutions to sentient constructs.** This is
deliberate, and it is one of the load-bearing facts about how the Last City governs
itself. **Steele** runs the Sentinels; **Alloy**, a veteran divine golem and one of the
oldest creatures in the city, runs the **Phoenix Guard**
([`factions.md`](factions.md)).

The two share a pattern with two different reasons behind it, and both reasons are
threats the city has actually met:

- **The Phoenix Guard cannot be *converted*.** Its ranks are mostly constructs and Earth
  Genasi because those appear resistant to being turned — an anti-undead order whose
  members can be raised against it is worse than no order.
- **The Sentinels cannot be *bought*.** They police a city with a syndicate in it. A
  bribeable officer is not a flawed Sentinel, it is the syndicate wearing a badge — and
  the Foundry's **Silas "Scrap" Copper-Cough**, filtering state material to the highest
  bidder from a government office, is exactly the failure mode a construct is chosen to
  avoid.

So the rule the city seems to work by: where corruption or conversion would be
*fatal to the institution's whole purpose*, put something in charge that cannot be
corrupted or converted. It is also the quiet reason **The Order** exists
([`factions.md`](factions.md)) — the coteries watch the parts of the city that *are*
staffed by people.

## The Archivists

The library order — wizards — housed in the **Crystal Tower**. **Stub.**

## Artificing

Where magic meets science. The artificers build:

- **Anchors** — the load-bearing idea of the whole setting, and the province of the
  **Serin** following (only those of Serin may set one; see the Explorers in
  [`factions.md`](factions.md)).
- **Witnesses** — converted beholders that extend the reach of the psi-hubs.
- **Memory Grubs**.
- **Flares**.

## Messengers — the psi-structure

The city's nervous system, staffed by telepaths at **hubs** who relay messages across
the Last City. The same order drives people around the city for a small fare and runs the
**sky lifts**.

## Wall Defense Force

The Last City's **perimeter military**, and the one institution here that is also a
class-bearing order — its Rift Drop-Troopers are written up as the future
`rift_knight` in [`factions.md`](factions.md), which owns the rank ladder.

**Vision** An unbroken perimeter; civilization held firm against the shifting void.
**Mission** Garrison the battlements, work the perimeter artillery and the automated
barriers, and close a breach in seconds rather than minutes — by high-altitude drop,
heavy polearm formation, and tactical spatial displacement (the **Blink-Drop**).

### The four cardinal garrisons

The Wall is held by compass quarter, each by a different people, and each drilled to a
different answer:

| Quarter | Held by | What it brings |
|---|---|---|
| North | Dwarves | Stonecraft fortification, runic shock-infantry, armoured sentinels on the mountain passes and the Dwarven Ethereal Depot |
| East | Hobgoblins | Disciplined tactical cohorts, phalanxes, mobile shock lancers drilled on precision |
| South | Kobolds | Sappers, trench technicians, and the crews of the high-yield fireball cannons and trap-lines |
| West | Tritons and minotaurs | Naval batteries and cliffside drop-troopers on the Ever Shifting Ocean, aboard armoured vessels (the boat) |

Cutting across all four: the **Rift Drop-Troopers**, elite shock troops who blink or
drop straight off a sixty-foot parapet onto a breach point.

### Defense architecture

- **The Great Ivory Wall** itself.
- **Fireball cannons.**
- **The moving wall** — built out of golems.
- **Stabilizers and the field** — maintained by followers of **Terim**.

The Foundry supplies the structural iron and magitech metal all of this is made of
([`factions.md`](factions.md) → The Foundry), including the requisitioned shock lances.

### Structure

Strategic oversight runs out of **Floors 46–50 of the Crystal Tower**, where logistics,
intelligence from The Trace, and deployments are routed across the perimeters. Each
cardinal garrison is directed by a **Garrison Commander**, who answers to the **Grand
Marshal of the Perimeter** (war room, Floor 50).

### Beliefs

- **The Wall is the boundary of reality.** If the stone cracks the Shifting Lands take
  the city; there is no retreating inward.
- **Verticality is kinetic superiority.** High ground is seized, held, and *weaponised*
  by rapid descent.
- **Interdependence over rivalry.** Dwarven stone, Hobgoblin discipline, Kobold
  artillery and Triton watch are one chain; no sector holds alone.
- **Speed closes the breach.** An enemy at the wall base is met in seconds.

### Etiquette

- Sector boundaries are strict; crossing commands needs clearance from Crystal Tower
  Strategic Command.
- Parapet duty demands absolute sobriety and instant response to the spatial sirens.
- Artillery and the moving golem barricades always fire *before* infantry deploys, to
  soften the landing zone.
- A dropped weapon on the parapet is a court-martial offence — every arm is tethered or
  bonded. (This is the in-fiction reason the Rift Knight's *Recall Blade* exists.)

### Notable members

- **Grand Marshal Vorn Ash-Blade** (Hobgoblin, Rank 6) — supreme tactical coordinator on
  Floors 46–50. Runs simulated breach drills and treats the perimeter as an
  uncompromising game of spatial chess.
- **Commander Bran the Tide-Horn** (Minotaur, Rank 5) — iron-willed commander of the
  Western Bluffs, on heavy coastal barges with an oversized anchor-flail for planar sea
  beasts.
- **Master Bombardier Tik-Tik** (Kobold, Rank 4) — hyperactive head engineer of the
  South Wall fireball cannons; obsessed with blast trajectories and experimental
  incendiaries.
- **Captain Thordis Iron-Pike** (Dwarf, Rank 4) — North Wall veteran with a runic
  halberd, who volunteers to dive off the high parapets and impale siege monstrosities
  before they reach the gates.
- **Strike-Sergeant Kaelen Swift-Drop** (Harengon, Rank 3) — East Wall jump specialist,
  in and out of spatial micro-rifts behind enemy skirmish lines to break a charge.

Led by those of **Terim**. **Stub** — and note that Terim's followers hold both the

## Spiritual

The **Order of the Open Flower** is the city's spiritual/agricultural order and is
written up as a future class in [`factions.md`](factions.md).

## Infrastructure

- **The Ethereal Way** — transit that works by skipping into the Ethereal and out again.
- **Mobcarts** — carts pushed around the city by psi conductors.
- **Sky lifts** — run by the Messengers.

# The underworld

## The Indulgent Sky

The city's syndicate, led by a **rakshasa** who hides his identity behind **weretiger
guards**. Its heraldry is four marks on blank metal — **a sun with clouds, a crescent
moon, three stars** — and each mark is a business.

An **incubus/succubus pair, Jacob and Jazmine**, works across *all four* arms, which
makes them the syndicate's real connective tissue.

### Slaves — the crescent moon

Led by **Dao**. Staffed by the **cambion Fereet**, Jacob and Jazmine, lamia, jackalweres,
and human bandits.

### Drugs — the sun with clouds

Led by a **satyr**, a high-level bard. **Oozes are the drug.** Jacob and Jazmine work
this arm; **gnomes run the drug run**.

### Gambling — the world

Led by **Zwill** (human). Jacob and Jazmine again, with **wererats** and **halflings**.

### Assassins — the underground

The Sky's own killers, kept **in-house**: internal enforcement, discipline across the
other three arms, and whatever the syndicate would rather not contract out. They are
loyal to the rakshasa first.

## The Red Sect

The independent assassins' guild — and **the Indulgent Sky's rival, not its supplier**.
The distinction is the whole tension: the Sky keeps its own killers *inside* the
organisation, while the Red Sect sells its work to anyone with the coin, **including
against the Sky**. Two professional bodies competing for the same trade in the same city,
one of which regards the other's very existence as a security problem.

**Stub** beyond that: no leadership, ranks, or houses written yet.

## Shadow's Hand

The thieves' guild. **Stub** — a third independent power in the underworld, distinct from
both the Sky and the Red Sect.

## Where this touches the game

Most of the above is fiction ahead of implementation. The exceptions:

- **Anchors** are canon mechanics (CANON §W structures/anchors) and the Explorers' own
  power.
- **The Crystal Tower**, the **Great Ivory Wall**, the **Slag-Fields** and the
  **Stabilizing Towers** already appear in [`shifting-lands.md`](shifting-lands.md), and
  the Last City hub is partly built ([`proposals/last-city.md`](../proposals/last-city.md)).
- **The Foundry** is Forging's home order and the design of record for the ore/refining
  half of crafting
  ([`proposals/crafting-and-professions.md`](../proposals/crafting-and-professions.md)).
- **Mercantile has no order, by design — merchants are just merchants.** Settled, and not
  a gap: there is no guild to join, no charter, no rank ladder. A merchant's standing is
  their own Mercantile skill and their reputation, which is the correct shape for a city
  whose market is *player-run* (GDD §7 — "NPCs do not sell the best gear; the community
  runs the world"). The Indulgent Sky's gambling arm and the Foundry's bribe-taking
  Requisition Officer are the *criminal* end of trade, not a home for the honest kind.
